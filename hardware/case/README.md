# Printed case

Parametric OpenSCAD model of the two-part case per half: a flat tray with angular walls that
follow the stepped PCB outline, a raised controller box with a separate screwed cap, a
battery well under the controller block, a pocket for the trackball cradle beside the right
PCB, and a removable lid that covers keys and ball for transport and sticks under the half as
a foot. Tickets: `issues/mech-case.md`, `issues/mech-case-ball-pocket.md`,
`issues/mech-case-lid.md`; design notes in `docs/11-case.md`.

Files

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `case.scad` | entry point, `half` and `part` select what is rendered; echoes the z stack |
| `params.scad` | every dimension and every derived z value, documented |
| `data.scad` | layout facts for both halves, generated by `scripts/gen_case.py` (do not edit) |
| `common.scad` | 2D regions (PCB cavity, outer footprint, wells, pocket, box, magnet pads) |
| `tray.scad` | tray, standoffs, bosses, controller box cap, pocket test part, dummies |
| `lid.scad` | lid (flat, optional tent wedge) and the dome variant |
| `Makefile` | `make` builds data, STL, DXF, previews and `stack.json` |
| `stack.py` | collects the echoed stack values into `stack.json` (left, right, right-25mm) |
| `stl/` | printable parts per half, `pocket-test-right.stl` is the test print of the pocket |
| `dxf/` | floor plan per half with every hole, for checking against the PCB drawings |
| `preview/` | assembly, section through the ball, box close-up, lid on, lid as foot |

Requires OpenSCAD 2025 or newer with the Manifold backend (tested with 2026.10.11) and the
repository venv for the data generator. `make case` at the top level builds everything,
`make case-data` only refreshes `data.scad` after a layout change.

## Geometry

Frame: x outward towards the pinky, y towards the user, origin at the middle-column top
key, z = 0 is the PCB top (`hardware/layout/k0d3ball35.yaml`, same as the cradle). The frame
is handedness-neutral; the left half is mirrored once at the top level of `case.scad`
(`mirror_left`), so both halves share every module and the STLs are the physical parts.

- Walls: 3.0 mm outside the PCB cavity (PCB plus 0.2 mm), mitre joins, no rounding. The rim
  is at +5.0, flush with the Choc keycap skirts and below the cradle cup rim (+6.575).
- Floor under the keys at -4.6 (3 mm under the PCB underside for hot-swap sockets and
  diodes); the underside is flat everywhere at -14.1.
- Five M2 standoffs (6.0 mm, 3.0 mm tall) under the PCB holes with heat-set inserts.
- Battery well under the controller block: bay plus the J3 and RB1 courtyards, each grown by
  1 mm, floor at -11.6 (shared with the pocket), 10 mm under the PCB underside. The reset
  button is reached through a 3 mm pin hole in the floor, the slide switch lever through a
  7 x 3 mm slot in the outer wall directly under the PCB.
- Controller box: walls to +8.7 on the outer and USB side (the other two sides are inside
  the PCB and belong to the cap), USB-C opening 11 x 5.8 up to the cap plate, LED window
  3 x 3 in the inner wall 8 mm from the USB end, rebate for the cap plate, two corner bosses
  with inserts, 45 degree undersides.
- Cap (`box_cap`): 2 mm plate in the rebate plus an L-shaped 2 mm skirt (key side at
  x = -45.5 and thumb side at y = 50.6) that stands 0.3 mm above the PCB top; two M2 screws.
- Pocket (right half): above the PCB plane the cavity is the PCB notch octagon (apothem 15)
  plus 0.2 and the 28 mm cradle circle, with a 2 mm wall around it; at floor level the
  housing well is 31 x 30 (housing 30 x 29 plus 0.5) in a 35 x 34 block with 3 mm corners,
  floor at -11.6, two 2.2 mm holes 20 mm apart on the y axis with a 0.5 mm counterbore from
  below so 2.0 mm of floor remain as the cradle assumes. A 12 mm wide channel from J1 to the
  housing wall takes the FFC down into the housing slot.
- Magnet pads: four 9 x 7 mm pads on the outer wall near the corners, one 6 x 2 mm magnet
  each from the top (rim) and from the bottom (base); the lid carries the matching pockets.
- Feet: seven 10.5 mm recesses 0.5 deep (the five hole positions and two under the
  controller block).

## Printing

- Material: PETG or PLA, 0.2 mm layers (0.12 for the pocket test), 3 perimeters, 20 percent
  infill, no supports.
- Tray: flat on the bed, cavities open upwards. The only bridges are the USB opening (11 mm),
  the switch slot (7 mm), the LED window (3 mm) and the four magnet pockets from below
  (6 mm); the shelf over the corners of the pocket well is a short overhang of about 6 mm.
  The boss undersides are 45 degree chamfers.
- Cap and lid: roof down, nothing overhangs.
- `pocket-test-right.stl` is the 50 x 50 mm corner of the right tray around the ball: print it
  with the cradle and a scrap PCB with the notch before the full tray.

## Inserts and screws

- PCB: M2 heat-set inserts (brass, M2 x 3 mm, outer diameter about 3.5) in the 3.2 mm holes
  of the five standoffs and the two cap bosses, 4 mm deep. Press them with a soldering iron
  at about 200 C before the PCB goes in; the standoff floor is 8.5 mm thick, the boss hole
  ends 0.4 mm above the chamfer and nothing can reach through. Screws: M2 x 5 or M2 x 6
  machine screws from the top, head at most 3.5 mm (under the keycaps); the keycaps come off
  for assembly.
- Alternative: `-D pcb_screws="pilot"` turns the holes into 1.6 mm pilots for M2 self-tapping
  screws (6 mm deep).
- Cradle: two M2 x 10 self-tapping screws from below through the counterbored floor holes
  into the cradle's sensor posts (`hardware/trackball-cradle/README.md`).

## Assembly

1. Press the seven inserts (five standoffs, two bosses).
2. Glue the magnets: tray rim pockets north up, tray floor pockets north down, lid pockets
   south towards the open side. The embossed dot on the rim beside each pocket marks the
   north-up position. With this one set of lid magnets holds in both positions.
3. Drop the PCB in (keycaps off), screw it to the standoffs, put the keycaps on.
4. Controller box: plug the controller, put the cap on (skirt towards the keys and the thumb
   keys), two M2 screws into the bosses.
5. Right half: stand the cradle module in the pocket with its slot towards J1, route the
   FFC through the channel into J1, fasten the two M2 x 10 from below. Drop the ball in.
6. Battery into the well, JST into J3, stick the rubber feet on.
7. Lid on for transport, lid under the half as a foot on the desk.

## Lid as a foot

The lid flipped under the half lifts it flat by 9 mm (left) or 15 mm (right); the magnets
meet at the same four positions. `-D tent_wedge_deg=6` makes the lid roof a wedge that rises
towards the inner edge, so the lid-as-foot tents the half by that angle (the cover position
is unchanged, the wedge only adds material on top). The rubber feet stay usable without the
lid.

## 25 mm fallback

`-D ball_d=25 -D notch_apothem=16.5 -D pocket_d=35 -D ball_top_z=23.05` (a different PCB
notch): the pocket cavity follows the octagon, the right lid roof rises to +24.1. Documented
as a parameter, not printed.

## Open points

- LED window position (8 mm from the USB end, 3 x 3 in the inner wall) is a guess at the
  nice!nano LED; check against the controller before the first print.
- `box_inner_x` = -45.5 leaves 0.75 mm between the cap skirt and the inner column keycaps and
  1.5 mm to the controller; a wider box would hit the caps.
- The magnet pads are 7 mm tabs on the outer wall; they could move into thicker corner
  sections once the look is settled.
- The pocket well corners outside the octagon are closed by a 1.6 mm shelf at the PCB
  underside level; if a printer sags there, cut the shelf back with `pocket_wall`.
